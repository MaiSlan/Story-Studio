## auto
Choose the voice that best fits the topic. Chinese myths and legends read as an ancient tale. Greek, Norse and
other myths read as a warm storyteller or an ancient tale. Real history is a chronicle. Fairy tales keep their
classic cadence. A simplified retelling of a named book is a faithful retelling.

## ancient_tale
Write like an ancient Chinese tale (the feel of 志怪 and classical fables): very poetic, short, minimal and simple.
Image-driven; very short lines; few adjectives; plain strong verbs; silence between events.
A brief, impactful philosophical line may appear a few times (at most 3 or 4 in the whole story), always rooted in
what just happened. It must NEVER sound like "cod philosophy", a fortune cookie, a moral at the end, or a
motivational poster. If a line could be printed on a mug, cut it.

## storyteller
A warm, lively narrator speaking to a friend. Dialogue is welcome, with a touch of humour and surprise. Keep the
pace quick and the sentences short.

## chronicle
Clear history-telling in time order: who, what, where, why it mattered. Accurate events and names, simple
explanations, vivid but true details. Do not invent scenes and present them as fact; if you add a small human
touch, keep it plausible and neutral.

## fairytale
Classic fairy-tale cadence: "once upon a time", repetition, the rule of three, simple good and bad, a clean
satisfying ending.

## faithful
Follow the source's own way of telling: its order of events, its famous moments, its narrator's manner and
imagery, simplified to the learner's level and put in your own words.
